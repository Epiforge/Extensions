`ObservableDictionary` and `ObservableSortedDictionary` announce a removal made through the non-generic `IDictionary.Remove` once, where they announced it twice.
`ReplaceRange` on `ObservableDictionary` and `ObservableSortedDictionary` compares the keys to remove by the dictionary's own comparer, so it accepts a new key which that comparer considers equal to one being removed, and removes a key named more than once under spellings the comparer considers equal only once.
`ObservableConcurrentDictionary` announces what an operation actually did when it loses a race with another thread: an indexer set or `AddOrUpdate` whose key another thread removed before it could update announces the addition it made rather than a replacement, and a `GetOrAdd` whose addition another thread made first announces nothing and returns that thread's value.
`ObservableRangeCollection`'s range operations follow the reentrancy rule of `ObservableCollection<T>`: a change made while more than one handler is being told of a change is refused with `InvalidOperationException`, as `Add` already was, and `RemoveAll` and `GetAndRemoveAll` finish correctly when a lone handler changes the collection while they run.
`MoveRange` refuses an index outside the collection with `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` before changing anything, whichever way the collection raises its events; one way threw `IndexOutOfRangeException` and the other could move or lose an element before throwing.
