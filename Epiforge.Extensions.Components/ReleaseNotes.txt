Comparing two values of a value type which does not implement IEquatable<T> for equality no longer allocates. Such values were received already boxed, unboxed, and then handed to a comparer which boxed one of them again to reach the very same comparison; they are now compared as they arrive.
FastInvoke is faster on .NET 7 and later, where it had been calling MethodBase.Invoke instead of the delegate it emits and had been using on .NET 6 all along. Invoking a constructor now costs 6.1 nanoseconds rather than 12.1; invoking a method costs 7.8 rather than 8.6, a smaller gain because reflection is closer to the delegate for methods than for constructors. Allocation is exactly what it was.
