ObserveMax, ObserveMin, ObserveMaxBy and ObserveMinBy now keep the distinct values they have seen in the comparer's order, so that when the value they report leaves the collection they read the next one rather than comparing every element again. Under a culture comparer over 4,096 elements, a change which evicts the maximum and a change which restores it together cost 1.40 μs where they cost 46.45 μs, against 3.16 μs for the first element of a descending ordering, and allocate 560 bytes where they allocated 992. The price is paid by every other change and by construction, and is announced here: a pair of changes which leaves the maximum alone costs 1.46 μs where it cost 0.28 μs, still below the ordering's 1.76 μs; constructing a maximum over a thousand names costs 449.3 μs with an ordinal comparer and 755.4 μs with the default comparer, where it cost 314.7 μs and 322.8 μs, against 954.9 μs for the ordering; and the query holds 88 bytes for each distinct value. Values the comparer cannot tell apart share one entry, which always reports one of them that the collection still holds.
