Expressions containing a try built with Expression.TryCatch can now be observed, by the graph and by direct subscription alike: the body is evaluated first, and while it faults the first catch block whose type the fault is supplies the value instead, that block being evaluated, and taking its subscriptions, only once a fault selects it; a catch block whose body is Expression.Rethrow lets the fault through. A try with a finally or a fault block, or with a catch block having a variable or a filter, is refused with NotSupportedException. Linq.Expression.Optimizer does not handle try expressions and leaves any lambda containing one unoptimized.
Direct subscription no longer reduces an invocation of a literal lambda whose parameter is read inside a branch of ?:, the right operand of &&, || or ??, or a try, unless that parameter's argument is a constant or a parameter. The reduction moved the argument to where it might not be evaluated, so the observation could report a value where the argument had faulted, although the graph and compiled .NET both evaluate every argument first and report the fault. Such an observation now reports the fault and builds the graph, which for a filtered view of a thousand elements costs 9,277.33 KB and 8,080.9 μs to construct and dispose of against 965.58 KB and 305.5 μs.
Observing a conditional expression on the graph no longer throws NullReferenceException when its test is false at construction and an invocation in its false branch shares an argument with its true branch.
An invocation of a literal lambda observed on the graph under a branch of ?:, the right operand of &&, || or ??, or a catch block no longer leaves its body cached by the observer, and attached to whatever the body reads, after the observation is disposed of.
