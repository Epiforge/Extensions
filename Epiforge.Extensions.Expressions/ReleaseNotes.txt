Projections, flattenings, filters, concatenations, groupings and counts receive the changes of the queries they are built over through a registry of derived queries rather than through event handlers, so each costs one 48-byte subscription instead of up to six delegates and releasing it allocates nothing: building and disposing a seven-query chain over each of 256 one-element sources fell from 1,593 μs and 3,980 KB to 1,297 μs and 3,048 KB
A query's derived queries now take each of its changes before any handler subscribed to its events hears of it, so a handler reading a derived query from within a change always finds it updated; previously this depended on whether the handler had subscribed before the derived query was built
