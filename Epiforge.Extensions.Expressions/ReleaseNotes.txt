Corrected: a member expression observing a collection or dictionary held in a field of a compiler-generated type announced the change to nothing, so an expression closing over such a collection stopped re-evaluating when its contents changed. This affected the default configuration.
Observable expression nodes no longer notify of property changes internally, carry a finalizer, or hold a lock for their deferred-evaluation flag, and no longer track a sequence for the dependents they notify. A node is roughly seventy bytes lighter, and a change to a value that a thousand expressions depend on propagates in a little over half the time it did.
Reading Keys or Values, and enumerating, no longer copy the whole result on every call for the group-by, individual-changes, lookup, to-dictionary, dictionary select, and dictionary where queries; the copy is made once and discarded when the result changes, as it already was for the others. On a ten thousand entry dictionary query, reading Values now allocates nothing and returns in about twelve nanoseconds rather than twenty microseconds.
