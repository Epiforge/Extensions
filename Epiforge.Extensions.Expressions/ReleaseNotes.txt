A key change in an ordered query moves the element's position in the ordering in place rather than splitting and rejoining the ordering three times, and looks for its new place outward from its old one before searching by halves; it allocates 215 B rather than 292 B and takes 0.44x the time in a view of a thousand elements and 0.25x in one of ten thousand.
An element changing group in a grouping or a lookup allocates 272 B rather than 592 B: comparing the element and its key no longer boxes a key which is a value type, and handing the change on no longer allocates iterators; it takes 0.77x the time in a grouping of a thousand elements and 0.84x in a lookup.
A grouping or a lookup removes the element which actually left a group, where it removed the first element equal to it, so of two distinct elements which compare equal under one key, the one which stayed is no longer taken out in place of the one which changed group; the group finds the leaving element by reference, without reading the elements before it, so an element changing group among ten thousand elements takes 0.47x the time and among 65,536 0.19x.
Each element of a view holds about 105 B less: an observation no longer keeps a lock it needs only while it is built, and an element occurring once in a filtered, projected, flattened or dictionary view no longer carries a list of its one position; building a filtered, sorted or grouped view of a thousand elements allocates about 104 B less for each.
Queries synchronized with a lock, a callback or a context, and not eventually, no longer take a lock around their queue of pending changes; one synchronized with a lock applies a change at once where it can take the lock without waiting and the change was not made within a handler of another query's announcement, and one synchronized with a context applies a change made on the context at once where none is pending; adding and removing an element costs 0.66x the time with a lock, 0.78x with a callback, 0.75x with a context and 0.61x on the context's own thread.
