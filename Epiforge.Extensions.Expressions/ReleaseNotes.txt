An invocation of a literal lambda whose arguments cannot all be substituted into its body, because a parameter is read more than once, not at all, or inside a deferred operand or a try where its argument could fault, is now observed by direct subscription as a let rather than by building the graph: each argument is evaluated once and before the body, a member read through an argument follows it as it changes, and the first argument to fault faults the invocation, as the graph's node does; a body the analyzer would refuse, or one which subscribes to anything but the one argument it binds, or does so inside an operand it defers, still builds the graph, because the graph rebuilds the body whenever an argument changes. A duration format reading its parameter eight times now changes in 208 ns allocating 193 B where the graph took 48.5 μs and 26,631 B, 233x faster, and an observation of it is constructed in 402 ns against 28.0 μs, 69.7x faster, the same as writing the substitution out by hand.
