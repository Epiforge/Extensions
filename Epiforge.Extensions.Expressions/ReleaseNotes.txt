An ordering whose results are empty now announces an element arriving among them as an addition of the elements it places, in order, at the start, and announces a change removing every element as a removal of those it held, in order, from the start, rather than announcing both as a reset, so a consumer telling arrivals from reloads, such as an ordering of a filter an element enters after the filter emptied, sees the arrival; an ordering whose remaining elements are all held back by a faulting key announces nothing when they leave, since its results do not change, and a reset of its source is still announced as a reset. Emptying an ordering of 16,000 elements by removing them from its source in one range now takes 967 μs where it took 99.7 ms with Collections 4.3.2, whose removal of the source's range shifted it once for each element, and allocates 4 bytes an element more for the removed elements it announces; filling an empty one costs what it did.
