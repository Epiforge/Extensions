The operators which observe a lambda of their own making, `ObserveCast`, `ObserveOfType`, `ObserveDistinct`, the parameterless `ObserveAverage` and `ObserveSum`, `ObserveToDictionary` given only a key selector, and on dictionary queries `ObserveCast`, `ObserveOfType` and the parameterless `ObserveAverage`, `ObserveMax`, `ObserveMin`, `ObserveSum` and `ObserveToCollection`, now reuse one instance of that lambda for each combination of types instead of building a new one on every call. The observer caches the optimized and compiled forms of a lambda by instance, so every call used to pay for an optimizer pass, an analysis and a compilation, and left a dynamic method for the finalizer: building `ObserveCast` over each of 256 one-element collections went from 8,433 μs to 231 μs and from 3.38 MB to 1.43 MB, the same time and allocation as `ObserveSelect` given a held selector.
The lambdas the queries derive from yours, the key pairing behind `ObserveMaxBy` and `ObserveMinBy`, the wrappers behind `ObserveGroupBy`, `ObserveToLookup`, `ObserveToDictionary` and collection `ObserveAll`, and the key-value wrappers behind dictionary `ObserveWhere`, `ObserveSelect` and `ObserveToCollection` and every dictionary operator built on them, are now built once for each lambda you pass and kept for as long as you keep it, so reusing one lambda over many sources costs one compilation rather than one per source. Over 256 one-element sources with a held lambda: dictionary `ObserveWhere` 21,046 μs to 224 μs, `ObserveToDictionary` 23,625 μs to 679 μs, `ObserveMaxBy` 17,208 μs to 577 μs, `ObserveGroupBy` 21,826 μs to 1,665 μs, and `ObserveToLookup` 63,764 μs to 5,041 μs and 16.61 MB to 9.18 MB.
`ObserveOrderBy` kept every key selector it had ever been given, with whatever the selector captured and its compiled form, for the life of the process, because it cached the lambda it derives from each selector in a static dictionary compared by structure and never emptied, and a selector which captures a variable differs in structure from every other. It now shares that lambda by structure only among selectors whose constants all compare by value, which is every selector that captures nothing, so writing one inline still costs one compilation in all; a selector holding a closure has its lambda kept only for as long as you keep the selector.
Dictionary `ObserveAll` and `ObserveAny` with a predicate cached their query under the predicate as given and removed it under the predicate as optimized. Where the configured optimizer rewrote the predicate, disposing the last observation left the disposed query in the cache, and the next identical observation received it and never updated again.
`ObserveToLookup` and `ObserveToDictionary` now substitute your selectors into the lambda they observe instead of invoking them from it, as `ObserveGroupBy`, `ObserveOrderBy` and `ObserveMaxBy` already did, which spares an observer with `UseDirectSubscription` turned off an invocation for every element.
